# Measurement Protocol & Error Log: Replication Checklist

> **Purpose of this document**: This log records the 61 real-world measurement pitfalls encountered during benchmark development on Krea-2 DiT and the 11 derived methodological rules.  
> **None of these 61 errors produced absurd values or obvious runtime exceptions**: all produced seemingly plausible numbers, quietly distorting the scientific conclusions. This document serves as a **mandatory pre-flight checklist** before launching any new benchmark run (including testing on circlestone-labs/Anima (Cosmos-Predict2-2B + Qwen3 0.6B) or future diffusion architectures).

---

## The 11 Non-Negotiable Methodological Rules

Before analyzing data or drawing conclusions on any diffusion architecture:

1. **The null distribution is never zero; establish it before inspecting the treatment effect.**  
   Always construct at least two null baselines: the seed floor (purely stochastic dispersion across distinct seeds given the identical prompt) and the sign/label permutation null on prompt directions (the directional agreement expected purely by chance).
2. **If a metric is formulated as an unsigned distance, it cannot observe a trade-off: signed projection is required.**  
   Unsigned distances (Euclidean, unsigned cosine distance, absolute differences) are blind to direction. If an intervention shifts one feature positively and another negatively, an unsigned metric collapses everything into a generic scalar "change". The signed projection decomposition ($A = \frac{d^+ - d^-}{2}$, $S = \frac{d^+ + d^-}{2}$) is essential.
3. **Always separate direction from magnitude.**  
   Never conflate the displacement amplitude of a vector with angular alignment across distinct prompts.
4. **A control that cannot fail noisily is not a control.**  
   A sentinel test must have a threshold such that, if the pipeline is inert or corrupted, it fails unambiguously and loudly.
5. **A gate that tallies misses and proceeds is a gate that stays silent.**  
   If an audit procedure (e.g., verifying text encoder tensor keys) detects missing layers, it must raise a blocking exception, rather than printing a benign warning and outputting "PASSED".
6. **The unit of statistical analysis is the unit you wish to generalize over.**  
   In our setting, the independent statistical unit is the **prompt** (the subject), not the individual cell or seed. Treating repeated seeds as independent observations artificially inflates degrees of freedom ($t$-scores inflated by $\sim 2\times$ due to high Intraclass Correlation Coefficients, ICC 0.66–0.95).
7. **Every table printed to the terminal must be persisted to disk, always.**  
   Intermediate computations must never exist solely in transient stdout buffers.
8. **Always report the test resolution floor ($P_{\min}$).**  
   With $n$ prompts, the minimum achievable two-tailed $p$-value for an exact sign-permutation test is $2 / 2^n$. With $n = 6$ prompts, $P_{\min} = 0.03125$: no effect can achieve statistical significance after multiplicity correction (Holm/FDR), regardless of effect magnitude. With $n = 10$, $P_{\min} = 0.00195$, restoring statistical resolving power.

9. **Blinding is a measurement, not a procedure.**
   Hiding a condition's label does not hide its appearance from an observer who knows what that
   condition looks like. Every round of human scoring carries a forced-choice discrimination test
   against chance, run on the same images, and publishes the result — whichever way it comes out.

10. **A figure is a claim, and it is checked like one.**
   A caption written by hand next to an image chosen by hand can assert something the data denies,
   and nothing in the pipeline will catch it. Every figure that carries a measured quantity is
   composed by a script that reads the measurement, derives the caption from it, and refuses to
   compose a panel whose measured role contradicts its label. A crop is itself a claim — that the
   thing to look at is inside the box — so where that claim is not verified, the whole frame is shown.

11. **The resolution floor sets how many tests the family can hold — count them before running them.**
   With $n$ units the exact sign-flip floor is $2/2^n$, so the largest family Holm can carry at
   $\alpha$ is $\lfloor \alpha \, 2^n / 2 \rfloor$ tests. At $n = 7$ that is **three**:
   $0.0156 \times 3 = 0.0469$ passes and $\times 4 = 0.0625$ does not. At $n = 10$ it is twenty-five.
   The number of comparisons a design can support is therefore fixed by the corpus size before a
   single image is rendered, and declaring the family afterwards means the verdict is decided by that
   declaration rather than by the data.

| **44** | Treating the relative Frobenius displacement over the *whole checkpoint* as a portable dose unit | The cross-architecture protocol matched $D = \lVert\Delta W\rVert_F / \lVert W\rVert_F$ between Krea-2 and Anima, computed over every floating-point tensor in the file. That works on Krea-2, where the norm mass is spread across the backbone (`Block_2` 21.5%, `Block_3` 20.5%, `Block_4` 17.3%) | Anima carries a single tensor, `llm_adapter.embed.weight` (32128 × 1024, the token embedding table), whose Frobenius norm is **38558 against ~101 for the entire rest of the model combined** — **99.96% of $\lVert W\rVert^2$ on its own**. Matching $D$ over the whole file therefore matched that one table and almost nothing else. The transposed preset came out with a rescale factor of **0.425**, i.e. a backbone edit 2.35× weaker than intended, and the "does it cross architectures" test would silently have been a test of two different doses | Compute the dose over the part the architectures share and the edit is *about* — here the backbone blocks `blocks.0–27`, excluding any adapter or embedding table. | **45** | Assuming a permuted control inherits the target displacement | `BLOCKSHUFFLE` is built from the same multiset of gains as the preset, so its displacement was assumed to be identical by construction — as it genuinely is for `RANDSIGN`, where every gain keeps its own tensor and only the sign changes | It is not identical for a permutation. Deranging which block group receives which gain changes *which norms are multiplied by which numbers*, and on Anima the shuffled variant landed at $D = 0.044077$ against a target of $0.045745$ — **3.6% low**. Small, and in exactly the direction that would make the structured control look weaker than the preset, which is the comparison the whole "structure, not magnitude" argument rests on | Rescale **every** variant individually to the target $D$ after construction and print the achieved value for each one. A matched control that is not matched is not a control, and a 3.6% dose gap gets read as a structural difference |
| **46** | Baking an edit into a bf16 checkpoint without checking it survives the quantisation | Applying a preset offline and writing the result back to a bf16 file is arithmetically the same operation the tuner performs at runtime, so it was treated as equivalent | It is not equivalent: the runtime path holds the patched weights at working precision, while baking rounds every element back to 8 mantissa bits. Measured on a synthetic checkpoint, the realised per-element gain deviates from the requested one by up to $3.9 \times 10^{-3}$ relative — exactly $2^{-8}$. At the gains used here ($0.02$–$0.11$) the edit is an order of magnitude above that floor and survives cleanly, but a gain of $0.001$ would be **swallowed whole** by the rounding, and a sweep of small doses baked this way would show a floor that belongs to the file format rather than to the model | Before baking, compute the ratio between the smallest gain in the preset and the storage format's relative precision ($2^{-8}$ for bf16, $2^{-11}$ for fp16, $2^{-24}$ for fp32). | **47** | A block-index parser that finds `blocks.N` anywhere in the key | `extract_model_block_idx` walks the tensor name for a numeric segment preceded by `blocks` and returns it as the backbone block index, with a name-based exclusion list built from Krea-2's own non-block prefixes (`txtfusion`, `txtmlp`, `tmlp`, `tproj`, `first`, `last`) | Anima names its text-adapter blocks `llm_adapter.blocks.0` … `llm_adapter.blocks.5`. | **48** | Porting a prompt corpus from the model that understands it best to the one that understands it least | The cross-architecture protocol reused the 10 stage-7 confirmation prompts verbatim on Anima, on the reasoning that changing a single character would break continuity with every Krea-2 result and introduce a prompt confound into the comparison | Verbatim is only neutral when both models parse the text equally well. Anima is trained toward keyword-structured prompts and degrades on long clauses whose word relations have to be resolved, so the shared corpus is not shared: its baseline renders start from a worse comprehension of the same string, and any edit is then measured on a degraded baseline. Comprehension becomes a silent covariate that differs by architecture — exactly the variable the matched design was built to hold still | Let the model with the **narrowest input tolerance** set the prompt protocol, and port upward: build and validate the corpus on the constrained model, then carry it to the more capable one, which can absorb a simpler format without loss. Size is not the criterion, format rigidity is. And parity is measured, not assumed: score prompt adherence on the named attributes of the baseline renders of both models before comparing anything else. Note the cost of the fix so it is not traded blindly — a corpus shaped to the constrained model's native format sits in a more canonical region of *its* prior, which may amplify or suppress the edit for reasons of its own |
| **49** | Naming a scaling law from a single dose sweep at a single step count | Three doses (100%, 50%, 30%) were rendered at 30 sampling steps; 30% was the one that converged without artefacts. The sweep has one step count, so the exponent on $N_{\text{steps}}$ is unconstrained: $1/N$, $1/\sqrt{N}$ and a step-independent threshold near 0.3 all fit the single observation equally well. The write-up then compounded it — the bridge arm was rendered at 30% while running at 9 steps, which under the proposed law should have been 100%, so the arm was under-dosed 3.3× by the document's own rule and its failure to converge was attributed to the sampling regime instead | A dose-response relation needs the threshold located at **two or more step counts** and the fitted exponent reported with its uncertainty. ## Replication Checklist for New Model Families (e.g., circlestone-labs/Anima (Cosmos-Predict2-2B + Qwen3 0.6B))

Before generation:
- [ ] Map all parameter tensors of the target model and verify numerical precision (bf16/fp8).
- [ ] Calculate baseline Frobenius norms and document target displacements $D_{\text{model}}$ and $D_{\text{encoder}}$.
- [ ] Construct the **RANDSIGN** control (random signs across identical tensors, $D$ invariant by construction).
- [ ] Construct the **BLOCKSHUFFLE** control via block derangement and apply $\alpha$ strictly to the permuted subset.
- [ ] Define a family of at least 10 subject prompts with identical syntactic structure and orthogonal color dominants.

During measurement:
- [ ] Harvest images by unique basename (no hardcoded relative paths).
- [ ] Verify integrity gates fail loudly if a single tensor key is unaccounted for.
- [ ] Establish the zero-intervention seed floor across all prompts.
- [ ] Perform seed-paired $S$ and $A$ decomposition.
- [ ] Measure across stroke/luminance morphological space, not solely on downsampled CLIP 224×224.
- [ ] Perform PCA and hypothesis testing aggregated at the prompt level ($df = n_{\text{prompt}} - 1$).
