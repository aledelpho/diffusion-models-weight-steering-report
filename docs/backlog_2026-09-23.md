## A. Zero renders — the data already exists

### A1. Decide whether the 555 contaminated images are rescuable — one PNG answers it

The whole HUD write-off rests on an assumption nobody has checked: **was the HUD composited onto
the image after generation, or was it part of the 1024×1760 canvas the sampler saw?**

* If it was composited afterwards, the top 1024×1280 is an ordinary render. Crop the strip,
  re-extract features, and the three rotation benches come back — 555 images, page 04 and the
  published page 08 numbers included.
* If the canvas itself was 1024×1760, the model composed for a different aspect ratio and the
  picture above the strip is *not* the picture that would have been generated at 1280. Nothing is
  rescuable and the write-off stands.

**Test**: open one contaminated PNG and look at it. If the composition fills the full 1760 and the
HUD overlays it, case two. If the picture ends cleanly at 1280 with a separate panel below, case
one. Then confirm on ten more, and read the `tEXt` graph for the declared latent height.

This is the highest-value hour in the whole list and it is one image.

### A5. Why `scramble_B` is a luminance pump — computable on the weights

`scramble_B` shifts ΔL by −40…−24 on the positive arm and +20…+32 on the negative, at the same
measured displacement as three arms that are harmless. The obvious hypothesis: sign-scrambling
`Block_6` changes the block's **net signed gain**, and the last block group sits against the
decoder where net gain reads out as luminance.

That is checkable **on the checkpoint, with no renders**: compute the net signed gain of each
scrambled tensor set and correlate it with the measured ΔL. If it holds, it tells you exactly which
alternative null to build (one that preserves the net gain) and saves a blind render round.
