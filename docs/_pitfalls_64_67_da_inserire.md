# Pitfalls 64–67 — from the negative-arm sweep and the noise-floor bench (2026-09-20)

Same column layout as `errors_log.md`. Four new rows; nothing renumbered. Pitfall 60 was
**re-committed** the same day (the 32-baseline acceptance gate was written on the file hash and
failed on pixel-identical files) — no new row, but its prevention column deserves a pointer from
any document that specifies an acceptance gate.

| **64** | A ratio statistic that is **blind to a component**, read as if it measured the whole effect | Each block group's sign response was summarised as `swing = HF_pos / HF_neg`. Groups with swing ≈ 1 were written up as having no high-frequency response, and the selection of the target group used `max(swing)` | A ratio cancels whatever is common to its two terms by construction. Two of the six groups raise high-frequency energy by 6–7% **in both directions** and score 0.997 and 1.030 — indistinguishable from inert. Worse, the selection rule was directional: an **inverted** knob produces a swing *below* 1, so `max(swing)` cannot find it, and `Block_1` (swing 0.739, sign-consistent on 6/6 cells in both arms) was missed entirely. The published sentence "only `B6` has a knob" is true about antisymmetry and false about high-frequency response | Decompose instead of ratioing: with $c=\sqrt{r^+r^-}$ (common mode) and $s=r^+/r^-$ (swing), the two components are orthogonal in log space and $c$ is what the ratio discards. Note $c$ carries **half** the noise of $s$ (averaging cancels, differencing adds), so the discarded component is the better-conditioned one. Select on $|\log s|$, never on $s$, or inverted knobs are invisible. Related to pitfall 63, but distinct: there the ratio had a floor, here it has a blind spot |
| **65** | Estimating a noise floor from **three** samples, then patching it with one measured on a different corpus | Every significance threshold rested on the seed-to-seed deviation of baseline high-frequency energy, computed from the three baseline renders the project had: σ = 1.15%. When a 20-seed bench became available it gave 3.3–5.2%, and the thresholds were widened accordingly, declaring half the day's conclusions fragile | A sample standard deviation on $n=3$ has two degrees of freedom and can be wrong by a factor of two or three in either direction — the original number was unusable, but so was the reaction to it. The 20-seed bench used **different prompts**, and seed diversity is a property of the prompt, not of the model: measuring the project's own prompts on 18 seeds later gave 1.65% and 1.83%. The imported σ over-corrected by a factor of three, and the amendment written on it reversed a correctly-calibrated pre-registered threshold | A noise floor is **measured on the corpus it will be applied to**, never imported. Fifteen extra baseline seeds per prompt cost minutes and are reusable by every later analysis; budget them before the first threshold, not after. Until they exist, prefer sign-consistency tests, which do not depend on σ at all, and treat magnitude thresholds as provisional |
| **66** | A **dose-scaling ratio** whose denominator sits at the measurement floor, read as a threshold effect | Comparing each block's effect at +0.050 against +0.200 (a 4× dose ratio), eight of 28 blocks showed ratios of 6–12 and were classified as requiring a minimum activation energy before responding | For 18 of the 28 blocks the effect at the low dose is **below the floor of the amplitude metric** ($\sqrt{4/6}=0.82$ for a 4-feature vector averaged over 6 cells). Their ratio is therefore high-dose signal divided by noise, which inflates without limit as the true low-dose effect shrinks. The tell was visible in the data: the ratio correlated $-0.49$ with the size of its own denominator. Restricted to the 10 blocks with signal above the floor, the median ratio is **3.16** against 4.00 for linearity — sub-linear saturation, the opposite of a threshold | Before interpreting any ratio of two measurements, check that the **denominator** clears the metric's floor, and report how many cases do not. A ratio that correlates with the magnitude of its denominator is reporting the floor. Two doses cannot separate linear from threshold behaviour when one of them is undetectable: add an intermediate dose rather than more seeds — the floor falls as $1/\sqrt{n}$, so matching a third dose costs about four times the cells |
| **67** | Measuring consistency against a mean that **includes the sample being tested** | Per-condition consistency across the six prompt × seed cells was scored as the number of cells whose effect vector projected positively onto the mean effect vector | Each cell contributes $\|V_i\|^2/6 > 0$ to the mean it is compared against, so the projection is biased toward agreement and the null is not 0.5 per cell. The score cannot be converted into a p-value as written, and the bias grows as the number of cells falls | Compute the reference from the **other** cells only (leave-one-out), which restores an exact $p=0.5$ null per cell and makes 6/6 worth $1/64$. On this dataset the correction moved the count from 16 to 14 of 28 — small here, but the p-value quoted for it was not defensible before the fix |
