## What this does to C54 and C56

* **Base rate.** Over the 32 seeds of this prompt now rendered (2 earlier + 30), the half-formed hood
  appears once: **1 of 32**, 95% interval 0.6–16%. For the 30 new seeds alone, 0 of 30 (upper bound
  about 10–11%). The watershed is a rare state of this prompt, not a property of it.
* **C56 is not replicated and cannot be, this way.** Its pattern — nothing at 0.01, the negative side
  tipping at 0.02, the positive side higher and as likely to lower the hood — stands on **one ridge,
  one seed**. The four predictions written for a second seed were never tested.
* **What stays true.** C54 (58 of 69 existing edits complete that hood) and C56 (the threshold
  ladder on that seed) are unaffected: they describe that seed, and both already say so. C55 (the
  active tuner is clean) does not depend on the hood at all.
* **Status.** The hood stays what page 22 calls it: a curiosity about one unstable detail, with a
  measured threshold on that one case. Any general claim about "an edit repairs a detail" would need
  a watershed that occurs often enough to be studied, and this one does not.

**For a next attempt**, a defect worth studying must first be shown to be common: the cheap screen is
a batch of baselines at many seeds, and only a defect appearing in a useful share of them can carry a
dose ladder. Alessandro's list of recurring defects (feathers on the arrow's point, a third arm from
two poses) is the place to look for one.
