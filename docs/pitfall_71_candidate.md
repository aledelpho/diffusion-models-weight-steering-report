| **71** | Designing a systematic sweep from the architecture without inventorying what the project already knows is inert | The perturbation atlas partitioned Krea-2 into thirteen regions taken from the tensor hierarchy — four depth bands × attention/mlp, plus `modulation_norm`, `txtfusion`, `clip_only`, `model_only`, `uniform_all`. Six amendments were written before the renders, each catching a real defect in the derivation, the anchoring or the plan. | None of the six asked whether any candidate region was **already known to do nothing**. `modulation_norm` was: the observer had asked for that zone to be skipped after seeing no visual change across innumerable prompts, and the corresponding widget had been removed from the tuner with a source comment recording that "every sweep over it produced images byte-identical to the baseline". The atlas rendered it anyway — 48 renders — and, worse, the region **manufactured a false positive in the primary analysis**: its two independent draws sit at distance exactly zero from each other by construction, which alone carried the apparent region effect from R = 0.391 (p = 0.278) up to R = 0.833 (p = 0.159). The Preset Loader later reported 84 of 84 layers applied, so the inertness is a property of the model or of ComfyUI's patch materialisation, not a key-matching failure | Before freezing any sweep, ask the person who built the instrument which controls he already knows are dead, and write the answer into the pre-registration. Then **keep the dead region and declare it the negative control**: an inert condition whose two draws must collapse to zero is the cheapest validation an atlas can carry, and a sweep that reports it as distinguishable has diagnosed itself. Undeclared, the same condition is a contaminant that has to be found afterwards and excluded — which is what happened. Family: instrumentation |
